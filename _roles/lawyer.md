## How it works

When the Lawyer would be executed, they may publicly choose any player,
who dies in their place. The Lawyer survives. Once the ability is used,
mark the Lawyer with **No ability**.

The chosen player *dies* but is not *executed*. So:
- Nobody was executed today. The Undertaker learns nothing tonight, and a
  Mastermind or Vortox "no execution" check sees no execution.
- A Saint chosen by the Lawyer dies without losing the game for good.
- A player who can't die (a sober Sailor, or a Tea Lady's good neighbour)
  survives, but the Lawyer is still saved.

## Design notes

This started as "whoever nominated you is executed instead". That was
too close to the **Virgin**, so the Lawyer now chooses who takes the fall.
Execution becomes a moment of drama: the town has to decide whether
executing the Lawyer is worth handing them a kill.

Similar official roles:
- **Virgin** (Townsfolk): nominating her can execute the nominator.
- **Scapegoat** (Traveller): someone else is executed in your place.
- **Slayer** (Townsfolk): a once-per-game public kill shot.

## Open questions

- **Is this an Outsider?** A free public kill is strong for good, much like
  the Slayer. It might need a drawback to stay an Outsider, e.g.
  - "…they die instead. If they are good, you die tonight."
  - "…the Storyteller chooses a player: they die instead." The kill could
    then hit a good player.
- **Choosing the Demon:** the Demon dies and good wins on the spot.
  Should that be allowed, or should the Demon be immune, like with Golem?
- **Drunk or poisoned:** the Lawyer's choice does nothing and they die.
  That confirms something is wrong, which may be a fun tell.
- **Bluffing:** evil players will claim Lawyer to avoid execution. The
  town has to decide whether to call the bluff.
- **Interactions:** Pacifist, Devil's Advocate, Fool, Zombuul.
